I've been kinda fascinated by what I believe is a common European approach to work.

For context, I interned at a German automotive giant, Knorr-Bremse. And like most of us, I've seen all the memes about Europeans seemingly spending random weekdays sipping coffee, cycling through picturesque cities, and generally not taking work very seriously.

But I think that's a misunderstanding.

What looks like a relaxed attitude towards work is often the result of being incredibly efficient during working hours.

At Knorr-Bremse, I had German colleagues who started their day at 6 AM and were done by 3 PM. I recently came across a post on Twitter describing a similar experience. The author mentioned that their European coworkers simply did not entertain distractions during work hours. No calls from family. No scrolling social media. No random gossip sessions. Unless someone was in the hospital, it could wait.

They understood that they were being paid to execute their responsibilities during a fixed workday, and they had optimized their lives around that reality.

And honestly, I think there's something all of us can learn from that.

Forget comparisons between countries for a second. Ask yourself this: how many of us can sit at work for 8 hours and not check our phones even once? No Instagram. No Twitter. No LinkedIn. No "quick" YouTube video. No random conversations that somehow turn into a 30-minute discussion.

Just focused work. I know I can't.

I'll tell myself I'm only checking LinkedIn for a message. Then I'll open Twitter to see what people are saying. Then I'll convince myself I deserve a quick break. Before I know it, I've spent more time context-switching than actually solving the problem in front of me.

The older I get, the more I realize that productivity isn't about working longer hours. It's about staying with the problem. Being able to sit with a bug, a design challenge, a document, or a piece of code and give it your complete attention for an extended period is a skill. And if I'm being honest, it's a skill I need to get much better at.

Coincidentally, I start at a new office tomorrow. So here's one thing I'm trying to change: fewer distractions during work hours. Not overnight. Not with some dramatic "new place, new me" transformation. Just gradual improvement. A little less doom-scrolling. A little less tab-switching. A little more focus.

Because I genuinely think that some of the best engineers aren't necessarily the smartest people in the room. They're often just the ones who can stay with the problem longer than everyone else.
