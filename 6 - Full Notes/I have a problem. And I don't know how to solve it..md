I am an embedded software engineer who does not inherently care about software. Oxymoronic as it may sound, it is indeed true. I like to think I care about electronics more, but truth is - I don't know anymore. I love voltage, I love sensors, I love processors, I love mechanical systems, and I love seeing them be controlled according to my own whim and will. I swear I do. 

But when it comes to software, I am not passionate about it. I never was. I wanted to get into embedded hardware, but life led me down the embedded software path since my days at the college robotics team, and I never looked back when I maybe should have. I got into college robotics, and this was in the pre-LLM era. I worked with sensors and MCUs, and MPUs, and a bunch of mechanisms for about 2-ish years. I then got into linux, and I loved seeing what code could do there. It was mesmerizing, to write something incredibly small and seeing it control something so vast and huge. IDK how to put this, but I believe I was more interested into the world that was being manipulated (the processor or the kernel), than the manipulator that I had with me (the code that I was writing/copy-pasting). 

And then came LLMs. I don't think there's someone who's been more devastated by the onset and rapid integration of LLMs more than I have been. Because I am someone who looks at code as a chore and not a tool. And to have half-hearted solutions available to me that are good enough to get the job done asap, is literally like the meme of giving a monkey an AK-47. 

See the point I am trying to get to, is that I don't know how to fix this. Will a Master's degree in embedded systems give me the chance I have been hunting for? The chance to pivot into hardware designing and stuff like that? Or will it give me the time and space I need to find a love for software, put more efforts into learning it all over again from first principles, and strengthening my core competencies? Do I finally get time to come around and explore the Linux Kernel and contribute to it via a meaningful patch? Will my parents be able to sustain through it all? I know I have enough saved up for a master's degree and living expenses that will be needed for the next 2 years, but will my family be able to coast through those 2 years of me getting back in a college and not earning anything? 

Or has time run out for me, and I'll be too old by the time I graduate with a master's degree? I am 23 now, I graduated in 2025, and the nearest entrance that I can realistically prepare for and attempt would be GATE 2028 (GATE 2027 is barely 5-ish months away, I am not clearing that at all, lol). Which will be 3 full years after I was done with my bachelor's degree. I'll be 24 when I start a master's, 26 by the time I graduate that. Who knows what will the world look like by then! 

I work a job now, and I am happy at it, but somewhere, somehow, the inherent flaw that I have shows up time and again. I use LLMs to converse with me through architecture decisions, write code, and debug any crashes that come along the way. Things work as I intend them to work, products perform as is expected form them, and nobody knows any better. But one code review with a teammate or my boss, and I am searching for flow of code that I am supposed to be responsible for in the first place. 

I am scrambling though the countless chats that I have had, assumptions that I have missed, and explanations for things that I don't seem to remember anymore. You see, I have a latent dread for the day that my fatal flaw of not really caring about code, perks it's head up in a manner that I cannot handle, and my world ends up crashing out because of it. 

I have a problem. And I don't know how to fix it. Everything else can be put on pause for the moment, but I need to sit with my thoughts, pen them down, and try seek answers. I know for a damn fact that running away form these thoughts is not going to help. I know ignoring it makes it worse than it already is, and I know enough about life itself that the only way to solve this is to face it head on, ugly as it may look like.
