Right, with that being said, this blog is an attempt where I sort of walk you through the relationship I share with this thing called money. I’ll recount my earliest experiences, what’s my perspective of it, and of course, what are my plans moving forward.

## The Need To Understand How Money Works

Right, so let’s answer something that’s fundamental to this discussion.

_WHY SHOULD I CARE TO UNDERSTAND HOW MONEY WORKS?_

Short answer — if you don’t, forget about anything apart from living pay-cheque to pay-cheque for the rest of our existence on this funny little rock that goes around in circles.

Long answer — let me tell you a little story. 15 years of my 21-year-old existence has been spent living in borderline homelessness. I’ll cut out the sad part of the story, and to give you a lot the gist — the family had a bright idea to mortgage the home to expand the business that they had, which failed miserably. 2010, a 2BHK in Mumbai, gold, car, everything got seized by the bank and sold off. 3–4 months later, 9-year-old me had to answer the door to the calls of Amex debt collectors as there was nobody at home, which is a nice way to find out that we have 15 days to pay 5 lakh rupees, or else they are coming for whatever is left next.

The point that I am trying to make here, is not that I have had a hard life and, boohoo, give me sympathy. No, the point is that I am someone who has grown up surrounded by a severe lack of money. I have seen, time and again, how a single hospitalization caused us to go back into debt; _(and no smart-ass, we couldn’t just “get an insurance bro” because the premiums were out of the realm of reality),_ how every time the school fees increased my Aai got a little more tensed, how we don’t go to the doctor unless your heart or brain stops working as 500 a visit means the groceries are going to be short for the month — you get it.

As I grew up, what made it worse was not knowing what was going on around me. I asked but got told that everything was normal — but 14, 15, 16-year-old me was a lot smarter than 9-year-old me, wasn’t I? I saw how dilapidated everything was. Covid made it even worse than what it already was.

But I had a couple of things under my sleeve that I never had before you see. I had a rebellious heart, a more mature than it should have been brain and phone with Jio’s data plan.

I was smart enough to know that money was the root cause of all my fucking problems (insert Jay-Z reference). I still remember it was after JEE attempt 2 (when I knew ki ye nahi ho payega bhai) that I decided it was time to do something about it.

I was always good at researching, you see. I grew up with Reddit and 4chan. Threads were my second nature, and soon enough, I was diving in, taking every bit and piece of information, I could get my hands on. I am talking about mutual funds, taxes, insurance, debt, credit cards, bonds, debentures, Government securities, Gold and other precious metals, shorts and long calls, options and futures, banking, depreciation and appreciation, compounding, inflation, historical catastrophes, the great depression, 2008 financial crisis, the UK government’s countless bailouts (PUT A WOODEN STICK THROUGH HER HEAD TO MAKE SURE THE WITCH IS DEAD, _if you know you know_), Japan’s financial plateau and the absolute state of lethargy, China’s rise in the Mao regime, and anything else that was under the sun.

My objectives were simple. **I NEED to understand how money works in order to ensure that I do NOT make decisions that mess up my future the same way that it messed up my childhood, and so that I can change this trajectory that we have been on** (looks like a fall from a cliff).

It was really simple in my head, I need to understand how to manage money BEFORE I earned a dime. So that when I did, I’d know EXACTLY what to do with every last paisa. My window was supposed to be 4 years late 2021-late 2025; so when life said _“Let’s bless this dude right here with a JOB a year ahead of when he’s supposed to”;_ I was ready.

I knew not what the time far ahead held for me but for the next 12 months was guaranteed — 2.4L (240k) INR in my hands, all before I graduated college. Sets a boy up real good as he becomes a man, doesn’t it?

## How Do I Wrap My Head Around The Concept Of Money

> **We Indians, as a generation, NEED to realize that we are _“ Children of the Internet ”._**

Now, what the hell do I mean by that?

Unlike in the past, the old guard (so to speak) no longer restricts our curiosity. There is nothing, I repeat, NOTHING like forbidden knowledge anymore. I’m damn sure that no matter how obscure your ideas are, or how niche your interests are, there is a blog, a forum, a subreddit, and a YT channel that talks about it. And this is where I stuck gold.

The biggest money problem I’ve seen around me has been debt repayment. Naturally, this became a starting point for me to explore. How does debt work, what’s interest, how does one go about repaying a loan, what is the mental challenge that one faces during the time that they are in the process of repaying what they owe, what does defaulting on a loan mean, how does that mess everything up — these were the very things that I searched on google. And like a ripple that runs through a pond when a stone is splashed into it, the net of things that I learned about kept on spreading by the day.

Right then, that should set you lot up well. So, what was the point of this? This blog, this rant or whatever you’d like to call this.

The point is, to spark a conversation. I’ll be real, there are people at work that I know who don’t know the difference between a Mutual Fund and an Index Fund. 25+ years of age and clueless about what to do with the money that they are earning. No real plan for achieving the dreams that they talk and talk and talk about till their gums fall off their teeth. Grown adults not knowing how a war affects their day-to-day life, why are freebies bad for the economy, why can the government never just abolish taxes, or why was demonetization such a bad decision… you get the point.

The only thing that goes on in my mind when I talk to them is that never in my life shall I be so deluded and clueless. And while I am at it, I might as well encourage you, my reader, to do the same. So yeah, take as much as you can from this story of mine, and most importantly LEARN about money man.

At the end of the day, the choice is simple — either you learn how to control money, or you let it dictate every aspect of your life. I’ve seen firsthand what financial ignorance can do, and I refuse to let history repeat itself.

This isn’t just about me; it’s about all of us. Too many people go through life not knowing what to do with their hard-earned money, and that’s a damn shame. So, if my story makes you question your own relationship with money — even for a second — then this rant has done its job.

Take what you can, challenge what you disagree with, and most importantly — **start learning**. Because no one’s going to do it for you.
